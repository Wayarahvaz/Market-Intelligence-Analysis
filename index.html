03 — CASE STUDY

90-Day Sale & Market Intelligence Report

The business problem: An Energy solution supplier to national power transmission sector was tracking at 18.9% of its annual revenue target with no clear answer for why. Sales data lived in three disconnected places — a legacy spreadsheet, the CRM, and the inbound commercial mailbox — so leadership couldn't tell whether the gap was a demand problem, a conversion problem, or something happening after a deal was already quoted. This report pulls those three sources into one 90-day analysis to answer that question directly: only 2% of inbound e-mails ever convert to closed revenue, but the pipeline already open is more than large enough to close the gap — the real constraint is that 72% of lost deals fail for reasons inside the company's own control (pricing and delivery timelines, not client decisions), and 88% of closed revenue sits in a single client and two service lines. The report turns that diagnosis into an operating-model fix — named account ownership, a real-time CRM as single source of truth, cascading sub-targets, and a standing hand-off from Market Intelligence to Sales on which regulated auctions and sector investments to chase next — closing the loop between "here's what's wrong" and "here's what to do about it."

Methodology: To develop this document, a data set well-built enough to describe sales tendency and behavior was necessary. This data originates from three sources: the Company's project-management spreadsheet, its CRM, and the commercial mailbox that receives inbound requests for quotation. Even with a solid base, a date filter was applied to develop an analysis based on numbers instead of impressions. The window is the last 90 days. This means the analysis does not aim to replace the sales methodology already in place; instead, it establishes a statistical process to prevent an annual revenue gap and a lack of visibility for the sales team. This document aims to fill the gap between the numbers and the overwhelming feeling of lacking strategy, raising the following questions: How are sales going? How are demand requests trending? How much did we sell? How much will we sell at the current pace? What is the current sales scenario? What are the prospects for next year? Which companies can we target? Who are our main clients? How much did we lose, and why? How do we avoid losses? What are the risks?
Conclusion: Based on 90 days of analysis, the Company closed $ 6.05 million against a $ 32 million annual target (18.9%) , a pace that — if unchanged — would close the year at 27% of target ($ 8.64 million in new contracts). Demand itself is not the constraint: 310 commercial e-mails converted to only 38 CRM opportunities and 7 closed deals, a roughly 2.3% inbound win rate, while the qualified pipeline of $ 57.8 million across 14 deals is more than enough to close the gap if conversion improves. The current scenario is one of concentration rather than scarcity: A alone accounts for 79.81% of closed revenue, and Onsite Services together with Equipament supply for 88% of it, so growth depends on diversifying by client and by scope rather than generating more leads. Of the $ 4.94 million lost in the same window, 72% ($ 3.5 million) traces to commercial terms and delivery timelines inside the Company's own control rather than client decisions, which is why avoiding these losses starts internally — with the account-ownership, CRM, and target-cascade changes recommended above — not with a market fix. On the market side, the same answer extends into next year: the named sector signals above and the 2026–2027 auction cycle (a record 6,091 projects registered for the first battery auction, over 6 GW of battery demand projected by 2035, and an approximately $ 2.5 billion transmission auction including the country's first BESS transmission lot) give the Company a dated, external reason to pursue the diversification recommended in this report — both by account and by scope. Read together, the target is not out of reach; closing the gap depends less on generating additional demand and more on commercial alignment. Internal approval cycles that run longer than the client's own deadline are themselves a source of lost deals, which makes a faster, better-prepared path for commercial and technical proposals on priority accounts a priority. Account diversification matters, but only if it comes with the technical and commercial structure to support it — dedicated, qualified sales staff who build client trust from the first proposal, rather than repeated rework caused by under-prepared bids. Shortening that revision-and-rework cycle is what frees the sales team to pursue better, more strategic opportunities, opening a new portfolio of accounts that will demand its own focused, dedicated sales attention.

CASE STUDY 02

Commercial Email Request Monitoring & Analysis

A pipeline to capture, clean, and analyze quotation requests received by email, built on Power Automate, Power Query, and Python.

The problem: The commercial mailbox received quotation, budget, and proposal requests mixed in with the rest of the daily workflow. Manual tracking was unworkable given the volume of competing tasks, and there was no structured history to support funnel analysis.
What this project solves
  • Continuous monitoring — automatic capture of new commercial requests, ready for bulk CRM entry.
  • Historical reconstruction — full extraction of the mailbox to size request volume and feed funnel analysis.
  • Reproducible ETL — turning raw data into an analytical base (client, thread, category) in both Power Query and Python.
Architecture: Email (Outlook) → Power Automate → Excel (OneDrive) → ETL (Power Query / Python) → Analysis (Power BI). Built entirely on the Microsoft Power Platform, avoiding the additional administrative approvals (Entra ID / Azure AD) that had blocked the earlier Microsoft Graph API prototype.
LayerToolFunction
CapturePower AutomateContinuous mailbox monitoring and one-off historical capture
StorageExcel Online (OneDrive)Structured table as the raw layer of captured data
TransformationPower Query + Python (pandas)Cleaning, client extraction, categorization, and thread aggregation
AnalysisPower BI / Pivot TableConsolidation of funnel metrics (volume, category, client)
Flow 1 — Daily Monitoring

An automated, always-on flow ("Monitorar cotações - Comercial") that captures new requests as they arrive.

StepActionTechnical detail
1. TriggerWhen a new email arrives (V3)Watches the "Commercial" folder of the personal mailbox; fires on every new item detected
2. CleanupHtml to textConverts the email body (HTML) into plain text, stripping markup and styles
3. FilterCondition (OR)Checks whether the subject or body contains keywords: quotation, budget, proposal, price
4. PersistenceAdd a row into a tableWrites Date, Sender, Subject, Body, Source, and ConversationId to an Excel table on OneDrive

The trigger points to a personal folder ("Commercial"), populated by an Outlook rule that automatically moves messages from the commercial list — avoiding the need for shared-mailbox access.

Flow 2 — Historical Reconstruction

A manual, on-demand flow ("Histórico - Comercial") used to backfill the base with retroactive data, so analysis could start without waiting months for organic capture from Flow 1.

StepActionTechnical detail
1. TriggerManually trigger a flowA single, on-demand run, with no automatic recurrence
2. ExtractionGet emails (V3)Searches the whole folder, no date filter, limited to 1,000 items per run
3. IterationApply to eachProcesses each returned email individually, avoiding context duplication
4. Cleanup + FilterHtml to text + ConditionSame logic as Flow 1, with coalesce() protection against emails with no body
5. PersistenceAdd a row into a tableWrites to the same table used by daily monitoring, unifying history and ongoing capture
Transformation Layer (ETL)
StepTransformation applied
DeduplicationRemoves identical rows (sender + subject + date), which can arise from overlap between daily monitoring and historical extraction
Client extractionSplits the sender's email at the @ delimiter, isolating the domain as the client identifier
Thread identificationGroups by ConversationId (Outlook's native identifier), more reliable than comparing subject text
Type classificationThe first message in a thread is flagged "New Request"; subsequent ones as "Update"
CategorizationKeyword-based classification into Quotation, Technical Proposal, or Other
AggregationConsolidation by thread: request date, last update date, negotiation duration, and interaction count
Technical decisions & trade-offs
  • Power Automate over Microsoft Graph API — the initial Python + MSAL + Graph API approach required Entra ID admin approval (Mail.Read), creating a blocking dependency. Power Automate sidestepped this because the Office 365 Outlook connectors already had pre-approved consent on the tenant.
  • Keyword classification over AI (AI Builder) — AI Builder showed configuration instability and depended on Power Platform capacity credits; keyword filtering was kept for this phase.
  • Grouping by ConversationId over subject text — normalizing subject text (stripping RE:/RES:/FW:) is fragile across language and formatting variations; the native ConversationId solves this deterministically.
  • Manual over automatic pagination — "Get emails (V3)" exposes no native pagination above ~1,000 items; extraction was capped at 1,000 items (~99.8% of the mailbox) instead of building a manual pagination loop, prioritizing maintainability.
Results

After ETL and thread grouping, the historical extraction identified dozens of distinct commercial requests, categorized by type and tied to the client's email domain — a structured base to answer volume, category, and client concentration on an ongoing basis, without manual effort.

Future improvements
  • Semantic classification via AI Builder or a language model, replacing keyword filtering
  • Automatic extraction of the requester's name and company from the email signature
  • Migrating the analysis layer to Power BI, linked to the CRM, with average time-to-proposal calculated
  • SQL-based ETL for more scalable consolidation and querying of requests
  • A machine learning model to classify email intent, replacing the thread-based heuristic
